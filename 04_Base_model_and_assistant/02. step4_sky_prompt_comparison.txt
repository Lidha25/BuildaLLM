Why is the sky blue?

When you look up at the sky, the light from stars travels through space until it hits Earth. In the center of the Earth (at the north and south poles), sunlight meets a layer of air that reflects most of the visible light back to our eyes. The air in between us and the sun acts as a sort of second atmosphere or “bubble.” When this bubble reaches our planet’s surface, it blocks some of the rays from hitting our atmosphere, which causes the color we see on Earth. This phenomenon has been known for centuries; it was named after the Greek god of the sky, Zeus, who had his own version of this phenomenon.
